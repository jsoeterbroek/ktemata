“A possession for all time”

A rugged offline-library cyberdeck with a very low power consumption: Raspberry Pi 5 (4 GB), a 7″ DSI touch display, a Waveshare UPS Module 3S with three 18650 cells, and a compact keyboard in a sturdy case. It keeps Wikipedia, maps, manuals and books on the device — no network, no account, no cloud. Charged by mains, solar or an axial-flux hand crank. The device manages itself from a built-in admin page.

The name

Ktemata (κτήματα) is Greek for “possessions, things owned”. Thucydides opens his History of the Peloponnesian War by calling his work κτῆμα ἐς ἀεί, “a possession for all time”: written down so that what happened stays available to whoever comes later. That is the device’s job.

The build

Computer
Raspberry Pi 5, 4 GB
Display
Waveshare 7inch DSI LCD (C), 1024 × 600
Input
Rii K06 keyboard with touchpad
Power
Waveshare UPS Module 3S, 3× 18650 in 3S, 12.6 V / 2 A charge input
Wi-Fi
Optional local hotspot for up to 20 clients
Charge
Mains, solar or axial-flux hand crank through a 3S regulator
Content
Built-in Ktemata Admin; no separate management stack
AI
Optional local document and ZIM search through Ollama and KiwixRAG
Hardware monitoring
ESP32 + 1.3″ OLED for battery, time and storage status
Time/date
DS3231 real-time clock with backup cell
Inner case
Custom parametric R6 cassette, printable STLs
Outer case
Peli 1200, IP67

KISS

The whole project is based on the KISS principle — keep everything simple. The core is the Pi 5, the 7″ touch display, the keyboard, the UPS Module 3S with three 18650 cells, the SSD and the real-time clock. Everything else has a job and nothing more: the status OLED reads the pack with the Pi off, the Wi-Fi is a local access point with no uplink, and charging is one sealed input. Extra boards, adapters and switches are another point of failure to debug when nothing else works.
