Power

What the R6 handheld actually draws, what its 3S pack gives back, and where the energy goes.

  • 4.0 Wscreen off, Pi 5 up, from the 5 V rail

Where the energy goes

Everything on the device hangs off one 5 V rail. The Waveshare UPS Module 3S takes the 3S pack (approximately 9–12.6 V), converts it to 5 V at an efficiency of about 0.88, and the Pi distributes it to the 7-inch display, the SSD and the status microcontroller. Charging comes in the other way, at 12.6 V / 2 A, through the same module.

States and runtime

The pack column applies η = 0.88 and a 0.85 usable depth of discharge. A 3.0 Ah pack gives 33.3 Wh, or 24.9 Wh at the 5 V rail; 3.5 Ah cells give 38.9 Wh and 29.1 Wh. The BOM fixes a 3.0–3.5 Ah range, so both are shown.

Twenty-four hours

Ktemata is a handheld, but a machine left on can be priced by the day. Three profiles are deliberately unflattering: a pure server role, normal mixed use, and a kiosk display nobody switches off. The hotspot row is the shipped Wi-Fi access point, with an estimated 0.4 W radio load.

Assumptions that could be wrong

  • Assumptions, not measurements: η = 0.88 for the UPS Module 3S converter, 0.85 usable depth of discharge, 3.5 peak-sun-hours, 0.65 panel derate, €0.30/kWh. The INA219 reports the pack directly; a discharge run is the measurement still to make.
  • Cell capacity drives the runtime. 3.0 Ah cells give 33.3 Wh, 3.5 Ah cells 38.9 Wh (+17 %); both columns are above because the BOM fixes a 3.0–3.5 Ah range.
  • 3S is not 2S. The UPS Module 3S needs a 12.6 V / 2 A charge source and a 3S solar/crank regulator. A 5 V USB-C charger cannot charge it.
  • Pi 5 soft-off is not off: ~1.7 W until the supply is cut. The UPS keeps the 5 V rail alive, so “screen off, Pi up” is a real state; only the UPS output switch reaches zero.
  • The figures are at the 5 V rail. The “at pack” column divides by η so the conversion loss stays visible.
  • The hotspot's 0.4 W is an estimate, not a Pi 5 measurement.
  • The heavy row is thermally limited. The Pi 5 throttles under sustained all-core load; it caps time at that power rather than raising the watts.

Reproduce it

Every figure on this page comes from one script, which also writes the charts. Edit the measured constants at the top and the tables and images follow. 

python3 work/scripts/power_chart.py            # tables + the white docs charts
python3 work/scripts/power_chart.py --tables   # tables only, no images
python3 work/scripts/power_chart.py --site     # the dark charts this page uses

The full derivation is in docs/POWER.md.
