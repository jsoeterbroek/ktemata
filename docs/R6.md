# R6 — flat-open Peli case with the display and electronics in the cassette

**Status: proposed; feasible on the published dimensions, but not bench-verified as an assembly.** The
keyboard/OLED lid plate, the cassette tray and the full-face cassette deck are modelled and exported;
the cassette walls are still the host's. R5 remains the implemented build. R6 changes the component
distribution, display and UPS; it is not a minor R5 part variant.

R6 puts the keyboard and status OLED in the Peli lid and the Waveshare **7inch DSI LCD (C)**, Pi 5,
NVMe Base Duo and **UPS Module 3S** in the base cassette. The display and Pi become one rigid internal
assembly. The display's DSI lead no longer crosses the case hinge, and the UPS's manufactured cable
harness replaces R5's custom soldered Pi power feed.

The received Peli 1200 has been physically tested: **its lid can be lowered onto a flat surface**. The
flat-open position is therefore the primary R6 operating posture. A removable keyboard panel is not
needed for the initial design. Screen viewing angle remains an ergonomic item to evaluate, but is not a
layout blocker.

## Architecture

```text
Peli 1200, flat and open while in use
├─ lid: rigid keyboard plate
│  ├─ Rii K06 keyboard
│  └─ 1.3-inch status OLED + its button
└─ base cassette
   ├─ full-face deck, removable, vented
   │  └─ Waveshare 7inch DSI LCD (C), glass facing up
   │     └─ Pi 5 + NVMe Base Duo bolted to the display's inner mount
   ├─ UPS Module 3S + 3× 18650, outside the Pi/Duo keep-out
   ├─ FireBeetle 2 + DS3231, retained in the base
   └─ Active Cooler and internal airflow space below the Pi
```

The K06 needs no hinge cable. The display and its low-speed control lead stay inside the cassette. Only
the small OLED/button harness crosses the hinge. Exact interconnects live in the separate
[R6 KiCad project](../cad/kicad/r6/ktemata.kicad_pro), not in this document.

Keeping the FireBeetle and RTC in the base preserves battery reporting with the Pi off while avoiding
the longer harness required by moving the complete status subsystem into the lid. The OLED/button-only
split needs a five-conductor flexible harness, a keyed disconnect and strain-relieved service loops.
Loose individual jumper wires are not suitable across the hinge.

## Display/Pi/Duo structural assembly

The display has two independent mounting interfaces and R6 uses both:

1. The four **outer M3 inserts** attach the panel to the full-face deck's 3 mm pocket floor.
2. The four **inner M2.5 standoffs** carry the Pi 5 and NVMe Base Duo as a rigid subassembly.

The stock inner mounting plane is too close to the display for the Duo. The starting envelope uses
**15 mm M2.5 extensions**, moving the Pi mounting plane from approximately 14 to **29 mm behind the
glass**. With the Duo's existing 12 mm standoffs and 1.6 mm PCB, its underside is then 15.4 mm behind
the glass, leaving a calculated **4.9 mm** to the display's 10.5 mm rear envelope.

This is a starting dimension, not a released screw specification. Measure the actual display, spacer
threads and connector keep-outs before ordering hardware. The Pi and Duo hang *below* the deck's pocket
floor inside the tray volume, so the deck's 3 mm base never encloses them; do not copy R5's recessed
7-inch lid-cover skin into this location.

Keep the Duo with the Pi. Relocating it elsewhere would add PCIe-FPC routing and signal-integrity risk
without improving the architecture. The display/Pi/Duo stack stays screwed to the deck and removable for
cell and board service, not permanently taped down.

## Lid and typing surface

The lid becomes a simple rigid plate carrying the K06, OLED and button. It no longer carries the main
LCD, its glass mass or the repeatedly flexing DSI lead.

- The confirmed flat-open position supports the intended flatter typing surface.
- Support typing loads through the plate into the host lid and the supporting table; do not rely on a
  small adhesive patch to carry repeated key forces.
- The K06's charging and mode-switch access must remain usable or be reachable by removing its retainer.
- Closed-case hard stops must preserve glass-to-key clearance under plate deflection, not just at rest.
- A horizontal display is less convenient to view than R5's upright lid. Test this in the real working
  position. If necessary, tilt the complete open case with a simple support rather than adding a moving
  display hinge.

The OLED is not expected to need cooling. Moving it away from the Pi compartment removes its exposure
to the main heat sources.

## Power architecture changes

The UPS Module 3S is compatible with the Pi 5's power requirement, but it is **not a drop-in HAT (B)
replacement**.

| Concern | R5 | R6 |
|---|---|---|
| Battery arrangement | 2S, two 18650 | **3S, three 18650** |
| Pack range used by the model | 6.0–8.4 V | **Approximately 9.0–12.6 V** |
| Regulated output | 5 V / 5 A | 5 V / 5 A |
| Charge equipment | 8.4 V / 2 A | **12.6 V / 2 A** |
| Solar/crank regulation | 2S / 8.4 V output | **3S / 12.6 V output** |
| Gauge | INA219 at `0x42` | INA219 at **`0x41`** |
| Gauge shunt | 0.01 Ω | **0.1 Ω** |
| Pi power connection | Custom soldered feed | Manufactured UPS cable harness |

The existing 8.4 V mains adapter and 2S regulator cannot charge the R6 pack. Replace them with the
UPS-specified 12.6 V supply and a suitable 3S solar/crank regulator. The solar panel and external hand
crank may be retained only after checking their operating range against the new regulator; the crank
itself remains a separate project and no crank mechanism enters this case.

The UPS's USB-C output has no documented USB-PD capability. Assume that the Pi will not identify it as
a 5 A PD supply until a bench test proves otherwise. Any current-limit override belongs in the installer
only after the manufactured cable and output have passed a loaded undervoltage test. The useful checks
remain `vcgencmd get_throttled` and a loaded voltage measurement, not the absence of a desktop warning.

The 0.1 Ω gauge shunt and widest INA219 shunt range give approximately **3.2 A** current-measurement
headroom. That is close to the pack current at full 25 W output near low pack voltage and can saturate
depending on converter efficiency. This is a telemetry limitation, not evidence that the 5 V output is
limited to 3.2 A. Voltage monitoring and low-battery shutdown must remain valid when current saturates.

The R5 charge-source divider is also unsuitable for direct reuse: its 30k/10k values produce 3.15 V at
12.6 V and approximately 3.315 V with a +5% input excursion. The starting R6 value is **47k/10k**, which
produces 2.21 V at 12.6 V and leaves useful ADC margin. Update the KiCad design and the firmware's
divider constants together, then calibrate against a measured supply.

Use three matched cells from one batch, preserve polarity and insulation checks, and re-evaluate fusing
for the 3S pack. Initial activation, charge behaviour, gauge sign and discharge cutoff are bench-test
items. No live pack work is implied by this feasibility document.

## Power and runtime budget

The 7-inch Waveshare panel is specified at approximately **5 V / 0.5 A**, or **2.5 W**, with its backlight
on. That is approximately **1.1 W more** than the 5-inch Touch Display 2 used in R5. The UPS output rating
is unchanged, so R6 has more battery energy but not more instantaneous 5 V output capacity.

With identical cells, moving from two to three cells raises nominal pack energy by **50%**:

| Cell capacity | R5 pack | R6 pack |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3.0 Ah | 22.2 Wh | **33.3 Wh** |
| 3.5 Ah | 25.9 Wh | **38.9 Wh** |

For a comparable first-pass runtime model, the following table uses the existing R5 assumptions of 88%
converter efficiency and 85% usable depth of discharge, adds 1.1 W for the larger display and uses 3.0 Ah
cells. These are **calculated estimates, not R6 measurements**; UPS Module 3S efficiency must be measured.

| Usage | R5 estimate | R6 estimate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Idle, screen on | 3.3 h | **4.0 h** |
| Reading / Kiwix | 2.6 h | **3.3 h** |
| Browsing + maps | 1.9 h | **2.5 h** |
| Heavy load | 1.3 h | **1.8 h** |

The expected runtime gain is therefore smaller than the raw 50% energy increase during screen-on use.
Update `docs/POWER.md` and its generating script only after R6 component measurements exist.

## Cooling

R6 improves **access to cooling hardware**, not the host's fundamental heat rejection.

- The UPS no longer occupies the Pi's component side, so the Active Cooler has a plausible mechanical
  location.
- The cooler faces into the cassette and needs an unobstructed intake/exhaust path; a calculated floor
  gap alone is not an airflow design. The deck's 54 Ø4 vents (two hinge rows and two side columns) and
  the tray's Ø4 floor grid under the Pi give the compartment a convection loop; they are internal
  circulation, not an external heat path.
- The Pi, display converter/backlight and UPS regulator now share one compartment. Grouping them does
  not remove heat and can raise local temperature.
- The 7-inch display adds approximately 1.1 W over R5's panel.
- The closed Peli remains sealed. Internal fans redistribute heat; the pressure-equalisation vent is not
  a cooling vent.

For reading and light interactive use, the Active Cooler plus designed internal circulation is a
reasonable prototype. For sustained heavy load, run a thermal soak in the final closed geometry. If the
host heat-soaks or the Pi throttles unacceptably, use a verified heat spreader/host-wall thermal path or
change the operating requirement; do not claim that additional internal fan openings solve it.

## Verification gates

- [x] **Flat lid:** the received Peli 1200 lid has been lowered to a flat surface successfully.
- [x] **Schematic structure:** the separate R6 KiCad project passes ERC with 0 violations; its exported
  netlist has 59 pins on 20 nets and passes the BOM-link check. This is design validation, not a bench test.
- [x] **Paint/stencil generation:** all three R6 colour schemes, twelve Strata SVG masks, twelve rigid STL
  prototypes and the two placed lettering blocks generate from the R6 probe. Mesh and visual checks are
  design checks only; they do not close the mechanical fit gates below.
- [ ] **Operating ergonomics:** type and read in that position; decide whether a simple whole-case tilt
  support is wanted.
- [ ] **Electrical bench fit:** Pi 5, Waveshare 7-inch panel, NVMe Base Duo and UPS Module 3S operate
  together on the intended OS; display, touch, NVMe and gauge are all checked.
- [ ] **Power integrity:** loaded USB-C voltage and `vcgencmd get_throttled` show no undervoltage; charge
  and battery transitions do not reboot the Pi.
- [ ] **Gauge calibration:** confirm `0x41`, voltage accuracy, current scale/sign and behaviour near full
  output and low pack voltage.
- [ ] **Measured mechanical envelope:** measure the UPS with cells/insulation, all protruding bodies and
  cable bends, and the display/Pi/Duo spacer stack before setting R6 parameters.
- [ ] **Lid plate fit:** fit the actual K06, OLED and PBS-33B; verify key-face height, retention, harness
  channels, rear tape contact and host-lid closure before painting the plate.
- [ ] **Fit template:** print a thin deck/panel template and a UPS mounting template before committing
  to the full cassette.
- [ ] **Service access:** remove the display assembly and replace cells without destructive disassembly;
  retain access to the UPS's initial activation control.
- [ ] **Hinge harness:** prove the keyed OLED/button harness through repeated opening/closing and lid
  service removal.
- [ ] **Closed-case protection:** verify the keyboard, OLED and display cannot contact under deflection.
- [ ] **Thermal soak:** measure Pi temperature/throttling and internal ambient under representative light
  and sustained heavy loads.
- [ ] **3S charge path:** validate the 12.6 V mains, solar and crank regulator chains independently before
  connecting them to the completed assembly.
