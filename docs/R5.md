# r5 — internal cassette in a host hard case

r5 keeps r4's electronics (Raspberry Pi 5, Waveshare UPS HAT (B) with 2× 18650, Rii K06) and
**deletes the air-quality sensor**; the lid carries the **Waveshare 7inch DSI LCD (C)**, not the
Touch Display 2. It replaces the printed exterior with an
internal cassette that drops into an off-the-shelf waterproof hard case, so the shell, the corner
bumpers, the grip ribs and the silicone corner mould all stop being printed parts.

```
host hard case (bought)
├─ base cavity ── foam pad ── r5_tray ── cells + HAT (B) + Pi on the floor
│                                └── r5_deck (screwed on top): keyboard + status OLED
└─ lid cavity  ── VHB tape ── r5_lid_plate ── Waveshare 7" (glass towards the base)
```

## The proposed host does not fit

The **Peli R40** cannot hold this hardware, and neither can the R60:

| Case | Interior L × W | Lid depth | Base depth | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R40** (proposed) | 193.8 × 119.4 | — (whole interior 48.3) | — | **Impossible** |
| **R60** | 226.1 × 136.4 | — (whole interior 75.2) | — | **Impossible** |
| Peli 1150 | 210.6 × 147.1 | 19.1 | 72.9 | Interior is smaller than the 233.5 × 178.1 cassette |
| Peli 1170 | 267.7 × 153.4 | 27.4 | 52.8 | Interior width 153.4 is less than the cassette's 178.1 |
| **Peli 1200** (default) | 236.5 × 181.1 | **30.2** | **74.4** | **Fits: 52.9 mm stack in a 74.4 mm base, 37.2 mm clear over the key face** |
| Peli 1400 | 300.0 × 225.3 | 30.0 | 101.6 | Fits with a lot of spare height (297 × 222 cassette) |

Sources: the Peli product pages (`pelican.com/us/en/product/cases/<model>`, interior + lid/bottom
depth) and the 2025 Peli catalogue (Ruck series interior sizes); figures converted from inches. The R40/R60 publish only a **total** depth, no
lid split — 48.3 mm and 75.2 mm.

Two independent blockers on the R40:

- **The display fits, but the lid depth does not.** The Waveshare panel is 192.96 × 110.76 and
  **10.50 mm** from the front glass to the PCB back; with the 4 mm cover plate it is **14.50 mm**,
  plus clearance. That is now inside the R40's 193.8 × 119.4 interior, but the R40's whole interior
  is 48.3 mm and it publishes no lid depth at all (the lid carries a snap-in TPU organizer).
- **The base cannot hold the boards.** The taller assembly is the HAT (B) with its cells: 35 mm
  above the floor, so the tray rim is 40.4 mm and the closed cassette **52.9 mm** once the pad and
  the 4.5 mm deck are counted (the keyboard is sunk into the deck, so it adds no height). The R40's
  *total* interior is 48.3 mm.

R60 publishes only 75.2 mm total for a 52.9 mm cassette plus a 14.50 mm display and lead fold, so a
split is at best marginal; with no published lid depth it is rejected. The 7" Waveshare module is
192.96 × 110.76, so the R40's 193.8 × 119.4 interior would just take the panel; the base height,
not the panel, is the blocker.

**Peli 1200** is the recommendation: its 30.2 mm lid takes the 4 mm plate and the display's
10.50 mm body (14.50 mm) with 15.7 mm left for the cable fold, and its 74.4 mm
base holds the cassette: 8 mm pad + 40.4 mm tray + 4.5 mm deck = 52.9 mm (the 11 mm keyboard is
sunk into the deck, not stacked on it), leaving 21.5 mm. Interior 236.5 × 181.1 gives a
**233.5 × 178.1 cassette** at 1.5 mm per side. The cavity is not prismatic: Peli publish the
section at the floor, and the received case measures **250 × 193 inside the open mouth** (tape,
2026-11). The deck therefore overhangs the tray — it is cut to the cavity at its own height,
**243.3 × 186.8** (`R5_DECK_W/L`, 1 mm a side at its underside), with its corners grown from the
tray's R14 by the plate's own overhang (R18.4) — the same offset the host's moulded corner takes
under the taper, and it still covers the tray rim. The overhang is plate only; every hole and
feature keeps its absolute position. See `HOST_TOP` / `host_cavity()` in `common.scad`.
`HOST` also accepts `"1400"` (300 × 225.3 interior) if you want the extra headroom and volume.
The 1150/1170 rows are recorded only to show why they are not options; `HOST` rejects them by name.

The sweep (`bash work/scripts/check_r5.sh`) reports zero-volume seating contacts: `tray:deck`,
`tray:pi`, `tray:ups`, `deck:keyboard`, and the direct `lid:display` / `cover:display` fit pairs.
The lid/display contacts are flat at the PCB seat (default host z 100.6). `deck:button` can produce
a non-flat yet zero-volume CGAL result from its coincident cylindrical bore; check an ambiguous
bbox with `python3 work/scripts/stl_report.py vol <stl>`. Everything else, including the remaining
`cover:*` and `button:*` pairs, is empty.

`HOST` in `common.scad` selects `"1200"` (default) or `"1400"`; `assert()` fails the build if the
host's lid, base or width cannot take the stack, so an unsuitable host cannot be printed silently:
it rejects the display assembly (lid < 18.5 mm), the deck under the glass, the keyboard stack in
the base, and a cassette too narrow for the keyboard pocket.

```bash
python3 cad/tools/export.py --r5                 # the cassette parts + the lid cover
openscad -D R5=true cad/assembly_r5.scad         # cassette + transparent host envelope
openscad -D R5=true -D 'CHECK="tray:deck"' cad/assembly_r5.scad   # one interference pair
```

## What changed against r4

- **No air-quality sensor.** `SENSOR_*` no longer applies in r5; the left wall is free. The
  firmware has no BME688 support or environment screen (the sensor was removed from the
  project), so **no firmware change is required** for r5; the r2/r3/r4 pocket is unused.
- **No exterior geometry.** Bumpers, ribs, edge chamfers, `outer_body()` and the charge plug are
  not printed. `docs/CORNER_MOULD.md` becomes irrelevant for r5.
- **The display moves to the lid.** In r4 the case posts carried the display and the Pi hung off
  it; in r5 the display hangs off a plate taped into the lid.
- **The boards sit SIDE BY SIDE on the floor, and both bolt down.** r4 and the first r5 stacked
  the Pi on the HAT (B) with pogo pins. r5 no longer does: power now runs from the HAT to the Pi
  on soldered wires, so the two assemblies are independent and each is fixed to the tray floor on
  four Ø8 posts (Ø2.2 pilot, M2.5). Positions and orientation come from
  `reference/first_fit.jpg`, measured through a homography fitted to the tray's own outline:

| | board | tray position | post top | above the floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UPS HAT (B) | 56 × 85 portrait | x 76–132, y 16–101 | 26.75 | pcb at 26.75, tallest point 37.4 |
| Pi 5 + M.2 BASE + SSD | 56 × 85 portrait | x 152–208, y 16–101 | 7.4 | pcb at 20.0, header top 30.1 |

  Both use the **same Pi-standard hole pattern** (58 × 49, 3.5 mm in from three edges), which is
  what lets one post design serve both: Waveshare's 3D drawing puts the HAT (B)'s own four 3 mm
  holes at exactly that pattern. The posts differ in height because the assemblies do: the HAT is
  carried 23.35 mm above its cells by its own holder, while the Pi's post stops at the **M.2
  BASE's underside**, since Seeed's BASE is back-mounted with the SSD on its underside.
- **Nothing clamps the Pi from above, and the deck has no opening over its 40-pin header.** The
  boards are held by their posts, not by the deck. The 12 × 81 mm slot that used to sit over the
  header was a leftover from the version that *did* clamp the Pi with pillars on its mounting
  holes (those pillars would have landed on the header, which is 3.5 mm in from the GPIO edge and
  directly over them); with the clamp gone it shielded nothing. It was removed, which gives back
  4.4 cm³ of plate above the GPIO edge. `assert()` now checks the invariant that used to be the
  slot's job: the deck underside must stay at least `R5_HDR_CLEAR` (3 mm) above the header, which
  it does by 10.3 mm.
- **Split plane disappears.** There is no printed split: the tray is 40.4 mm, the deck is a 4.5 mm
  plate on top, and the lid plate is independent. The rim dropped from 46.0 to 40.4 mm because it
  is now derived from the measured envelopes (35 mm HAT + cells, 30 mm Pi sandwich) rather than
  from a pogo-pin stack: 3 mm of air over the taller assembly.
- **Charge input moves to the deck's right rail.** The host is never drilled: the 5.5 × 2.1 mm
  socket's barrel drops through the deck, its nut lands in the channel between the Pi's port edge
  and the tray wall, and its plug goes in from above. Charging therefore happens with the lid
  open; with the plug out only the charge socket's own bezel stands proud, inside the 37.2 mm
  between the key/deck face and the flush display/cover front. (r4 put the socket through its printed top wall, which no longer exists.)

## The lid cover: the recessed 7" panel

`r5_lid_plate` is a flat 4 mm plate that only holds the display. `r5_lid_cover` is the finished
lid: a full-height panel with the **7"** Waveshare display sunk into it, not a display sitting on
a thin plate inside a raised rim. The glass and the entire broad front face are coplanar.
See the [dimensioned section sketch](r5_lid_cover_recess_section.svg).

| | `r5_lid_plate` | `r5_lid_cover` |
|---|---|---|
| size | 205.0 × 122.8 × 4 mm | 233.5 × 178.1 × 14.5 mm (the whole lid interior) |
| holds the panel by | 4 × M3 into its rear inserts | the same 4 × M3 through a 4 mm rear mounting skin |
| display recess | none | 10.50 mm deep; glass flush with the full front face |
| power button | none | none — it is on the deck now |
| glass height above the host floor | 90.1 mm | 90.1 mm |

The cover's model datums are rear/tape face `z = 0`, PCB seat `z = COVER_T = 4.00`, and glass plus
broad front `z = COVER_FRONT_Z = 14.50`. Keeping the rear plane fixed preserves the VHB/host
interface, display position and closed-case clearance; the old raised rim is simply absorbed into
a solid front panel. Print lid/tape face down as before: the recess opens upwards and every wall
is vertical, so it needs no supports.

**r5 hangs the Waveshare 7inch DSI LCD (C), not the 5" TD2 of r4.** The panel is
**192.96 × 110.76 mm** with R8 corners and a **154.88 × 86.72** active area (top 11.00, bottom
13.04, sides 19.04). Its front glass is **10.50 mm** above the PCB back; four **M3** threaded
inserts sit 9.54 mm behind the glass (a 126.25 × 65.49 span, not symmetric), and four unused
58 × 49 M2.5 Pi standoffs protrude 3.5 mm behind the PCB. The 5" numbers in the r4 tables do not
apply to r5.

**The panel runs landscape, and that is forced, not chosen.** 192.96 mm is longer than the
cassette's 178.1 mm length, so the long axis has to go along x's 233.5 mm. It is **centred in both
axes**, which leaves 20.27 mm of plate either side and 33.67 mm above and below.

**The power button moved to the deck.** A centred 7" panel leaves a **33.7 mm** band at the latch
end (the 5" layout left 72.6), and a 16 mm IP67 button needs 16.2 + 2 × 9 = **34.2 mm**. The two
cannot share a 178.1 mm lid, so `R5_BTN_*` puts the same 16 mm button on the deck's right rail,
over free floor at the latch end, with its two leads going to the Pi's **J2** pads (the Pi 5's
external power button). The plate is the whole mount: bore only, no boss into the tray cavity.

Three things are worth knowing before printing it:

- **The panel is held by four DIN 7991 M3 × 6 screws, not by a lip.** The module outline *is* its
  glass outline (192.96 × 110.76), so there is no frame to trap. The screws pass through the 4 mm
  rear skin into the inserts, with blind 3.5 mm reliefs for the M2.5 Pi standoffs. Their 6 mm
  diameter, 1.7 mm high heads sit in 90° countersinks on the tape side, leaving the broad front and
  tape face flat. M3 × 6 gives ≈1.04 mm insert engagement; **M3 × 8 can bottom out**. The deck uses
  seven of the same DIN 7991 screws in M3 × 10. Both need a 2 mm hex key.
- **The display is inside the front panel, not on it.** The blind recess is 10.50 mm deep and its
  opening is **193.96 × 111.76 mm**: the glass outline plus 0.50 mm clearance per side. Its floor
  is the PCB seat at `z = 4.00`; its top and the surrounding front face are both at `z = 14.50`.
  The image is 154.88 × 86.72 on a 192.96 × 110.76 module, so a 19.04 / 11.00 / 13.04 mm glass
  border remains and no printed edge crosses the picture.
- **The DSI notch is on the right, aligned with the cable openings.** The Waveshare's 15-pin DSI
  socket is on the right-hand rear PCB. On the default host the 46 mm cover notch runs
  `x 137.87..183.87`, centred at **160.87**, 44.12 mm right of the old centred notch. That centre
  fits both the deck opening (`x 150..203.4`) and tray rim notch (`x 61.75..171.75`), with 8 mm
  half-width allowed for the FPC. `R5_CABLE_X = 140.60` is measured from the panel's left edge;
  `R5_CABLE_W` is its width and the depth follows from `PANEL_Y`. The 4-pin 5 V/I²C lead is routed
  inside (use a longer lead than the included 6 cm).

`-D LID="plate"` shows the simpler plate in the assembly instead of the cover:

```bash
openscad -D R5=true cad/assembly_r5.scad                          # cover (shipped)
openscad -D R5=true -D LID='"plate"' cad/assembly_r5.scad         # flat plate instead
```

## Decisions and their risks

**The host is not drilled; the charge socket is in the deck.** r5 needs the 8.4 V lead to reach
the electronics, and the cheapest place to land it is the deck you already remove for service.
The same 5.5 × 2.1 mm panel socket r4 put through its printed wall now sits in the deck's right
rail (`R5_CHG_*`): Ø9.5 through the 4.5 mm plate, bezel on the painted face, nut on the underside,
in the empty channel between the Pi's port edge and the tray wall. The Peli case keeps its IP
rating and there is no gasket to seat. The cost: **the plug can only be fitted with the lid
open** — the flush display/cover front is 37.2 mm above the key/deck face when shut — so a closed-case 24/7 solar
installation cannot charge. If that matters, the alternatives are a sealed bulkhead socket
through the host wall (drilling it; sealed against water only if the socket is a proper
panel-mount with its own O-ring) or a wireless receiver in the tray; neither is modelled.

**The lid plate is taped, not screwed.** Screwing it to the lid would put four holes through the
host's lid — worse for water resistance than tape, and the lid is a thin single-skin moulding
with no bosses. VHB tape on a clean, degreased lid face is strong enough for the panel's mass;
it is not removable without a new strip.

**The keyboard is sunk into an open recess, and the deck prints flipped.** The K06 is 11 mm
thick; the deck plate is 4.5 mm. Left as a pocket, the body stood 11 mm proud. It is sunk so the
keys finish level with the deck's top face:

| | |
|---|---|
| recess depth below the plate | 11 mm — the body's full thickness (`R5_KB_SINK` = `KB_BODY[2]`) |
| body resting plane | z 33.9 (`R5_KB_WELL_BOT`) = the top of the two seat ledges |
| keys, top face | z 44.9 = the deck's top face, **0 mm proud** |
| plan clearance | 0.3 mm per side (`R5_KB_POCKET` is 151.6 × 59.6 for a 151 × 59 body) |
| plan corners | **R6.4** (`R5_KB_CORNER`), measured off the K06 manual's outline drawing |

**The cut follows the keyboard's rounded corners.** The K06's shell does not have square corners,
so a square hole leaves four wedges of air where the shell should be — and the plate's corners
stick into the keyboard's own outline at those points. `R5_KB_CORNER` comes from the outline
drawing on page 2 of the K06 manual: the same drawing that gives 151 × 59 (746 px / 151 mm and
290 px / 59 mm agree to 0.5 %), with a circle fitted to a corner arc giving r = 6.4 mm (rms
0.2 px). The cut radius is that plus the printed clearance.

**There is no floor under the keyboard.** It used to have a 1.2 mm one, and that floor was the
whole support problem: printed top-face down it is a 151 × 59 mm ceiling, which the slicer filled
with ~4100 s of tree support. The recess is open instead: a 1.8 mm wall ring hangs below the plate
down to the seat ledges, and the keyboard rests on **two ledges, one along each long wall** —
`R5_KB_SEAT` = 2.5 mm wide, flat on top at the resting plane, with a 45° underside chamfered back
to the wall so nothing overhangs the bed. The K06's shell carries its rim on exactly those two
edges, which is also where r4's support frame touched it.

**Nothing may stand inside the 11 mm column.** That was a real bug in the previous version: two
full-height "carry ribs" stood *inside* the keyboard's own volume, and the plate cut sheared their
tops off at the plate's underside — so the body landed 5.5 mm below the top face, not 11, and the
keys sat 5.5 mm proud. The seat now hangs *below* the resting plane, so the body drops the full
11 mm. A band spanning the pocket instead would have been a 59.6 mm unsupported bridge at the top
of the print, which is why the seat is a ledge on each wall rather than a rib across the middle.

Sliced on the project's own profile (Bambu P1S, PETG, 0.3 mm layers, tree(auto)). Both rows
predate the seat ledges — they measured the floor removal and the 4.5 mm plate, so treat the
second as an upper bound: the ledges add 2 × 2.5 × 140 × 2.5 mm of material and remove nothing
that needed support.

| deck | filament | support | bridge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 mm plate, well with a floor | 121.9 g | 4127 s | 1640 s |
| 4.5 mm plate, open recess + full-height ribs (previous) | 107.6 g | 483 s | 1234 s |

The OLED cut costs ~1.1 g of plate: the visible hole went from 35.8 × 34.8 mm through the
full 4.5 mm (5.6 cm³) to a 36.8 × 25.3 mm window over a 38.3 × 36.5 mm × 1.2 mm pocket (4.75 cm³),
so the deck keeps a 1.2 mm seat behind the bezel at the ends. Support does not change: printed
top-face down the seat is the lowest material and the pocket opens upward.

One consequence to respect: **the deck must be printed with its top face down.** The recess hangs
below the plate, so printed plate-down its walls would be floating islands. Flipped, the top face
is the bed, every wall is vertical and the only overhang is the 2.5 mm ledge land at the top of
the print. The fan grille is recessed flush for the same reason — bars standing proud of the top
face would be pressed into the bed.

**The tray walls carry windows, and they are solid.** Both are worth knowing before printing,
because a thin wall on a tray that only locates and closes looks like a modelling bug:

| | |
|---|---|
| wall section in the STL | 2.6 mm solid, one wall (`R5_WALL`) |
| window band | z 10.4–33.95 (`WIN_Z0`/`WIN_Z1`), 23.55 mm tall |
| what stays | 8 mm band above the floor, 12 mm rim band under the deck bosses (M3 insert bores start at 34.4), the 14 mm corners, and a solid zone round all seven bosses |
| openings | ~24 mm wide, 6 mm ribs between them; head is a 45 deg gable |
| switch | `-D WIN=false` prints the tray with solid walls |
| saving | shipped profile: tray 98.8 → 89.7 g (−9 %, −11 cm³); 3-loop profile: 233.0 → 206.3 g (−11 %, −21 cm³) |

The walls do three jobs — locate the cassette in the host, carry the seven deck bosses, close the
tray. Nothing hangs off them: both board assemblies and the cells rest on the floor, so a wall only has to stay
stiff in its own plane, and the ribs do that. A run shorter than 12 mm is left solid.

The **head shape is a measured choice.** With the project's own profile (Bambu P1S, PETG, 0.3 mm
layers, tree(auto)) on the identical tray, support filament for the whole part:

| head | support filament |
|---|---|
| flat 24 mm ceiling | 4616 mm |
| semicircular arch | 3205 mm |
| **45 deg gable** | **497 mm** |

A flat ceiling is an unsupported span; a semicircle still overhangs beside the wall until its
slope shallows; 45 deg is this project's printable-overhang limit, so the gable prints as two
self-supporting slopes and only its last two layers bridge.

**Print profile: three walls, a top shell and 15 % infill.** The STL above is a solid wall, so if
your slicer shows a hollow one, the *profile* is doing it. `stl/ktemata_r5_cass.3mf` used to ship
`wall_loops=1`, `top_shell_layers=0`, `sparse_infill_density=0%`, which sliced the tray as two
0.55 mm lines with 1.04 mm of air between them and a floor only 1.2 mm thick. It now carries
`wall_loops=3`, `top_shell_layers=4` and `sparse_infill_density=15%`, which slices the 2.6 mm wall as
one continuous band. Measured on the tray, same mesh:

| profile | wall at mid-height | tray |
|---|---|---|
| 1 loop, no top shell, 0 % (as shipped) | two lines, 1.04 mm air between | 98.8 g |
| 2 loops, top shell, 0 % | two lines, 0.52 mm air between | 183.1 g |
| **3 loops, top shell, 15 %** | **one solid 2.6 mm band** | **206.3 g** |

Two loops are not enough: 2 × 0.55 mm leaves 0.52 mm down the middle of a 2.6 mm wall, and the gap
only fills if there is infill for it to anchor to. All three plates together cost 195.6 g as
shipped and 365.0 g corrected — that is the price of walls and skins that are actually closed, not
a modelling change.

**Deck cooling.** The deck used to carry a bank of five 2.4 mm slots at the right-hand end. They
sat in the wrong place — their y span (101–108.4) crossed the keyboard pocket's hinge wall
(100.7–102.5), so they cut into the pocket instead of venting — and a slot bank moves little air.
They are gone. In their place is a **38 mm round opening for a 40 mm fan** in the free top-left
corner — centre (44, 38) — with a **seven-bar printed grille** recessed flush into the deck. A
second opening was modelled in the top-right corner (189.5, 38) but is **off by default**
(`DECK_FANS=1`): it bought little, and its rim was what forced the hinge opening to be cut
shallower than the Pi's connectors needed:

| | |
|---|---|
| `DECK_FANS` | `1` (default: top-left at (44, 38)); `2` adds the top-right one at (189.5, 38). With `2`, the hinge opening's depth follows the right fan's rim |
| opening | Ø38, so a 40 mm fan's blades clear it by 1 mm all round |
| mounting | M3 clearance holes on the fan's 32 mm square pattern |
| grille | 7 bars, 1.4 mm wide, 1.2 mm ribs, printed as part of the deck |

The corner is clear of everything: the keyboard recess starts at y = 102.5, which leaves 25 mm of
plate. The bars bridge the opening so the plate keeps its
stiffness where a 38 mm hole removed its middle, and every bar is a straight bridge — no support
needed. The opening is a path for a fan, **not** a cooling solution on its own — see **Heat**
below, which has not changed.

**Heat.** The host is closed and sealed, and the Pi 5 idles around 5 W and peaks near 12 W. The
tray's floor vents only move air inside the host; there is no path through the host wall, and the
Peli's own pressure-relief valve is a 3 micron hydrophobic vent (it equalises pressure, it does
not convect). Expect the SoC to sit at its throttle point under sustained load. If that is not
acceptable, the honest options are a vented replacement for the relief-valve plug or a small
heatsink coupled to the host wall — not extra holes.

**The power button is on the deck, and that is a consequence of centring the panel.** The 16 mm
IP67 button was designed into the lid's latch band; a centred 7" panel leaves 33.7 mm there and the
button needs 34.2 mm, so the two cannot coexist in a 178.1 mm lid. `R5_BTN_X/Y` put it on the
deck's right rail at (213.0, 160.1) — 9.2 mm clear of the keyboard skirt, past every board and
cell (they end at y 101), and 18.1 mm off the latch edge. The plate is the mount: a Ø16.2 bore and
nothing else, since a boss hanging into the tray cavity would foul the leads. Two wires go to the
Pi's **J2** pads, which is the Pi 5's external power-button input (`assert()`s hold the rail
clearances). The nut on the plate's underside is a zero-volume tangent in the sweep, as intended.

**The keyboard is only on the deck, so it is not usable with the case shut.** That matches r4:
the face window was the typing surface. With r5 you open the lid, the display is in the lid over
the keyboard. The K06's 2.4 GHz/BT link means the keyboard can be lifted out and used at a
distance from the deck if you want.

**The keyboard is sunk level with the deck, not standing on it.** r4 dropped the K06 into a pocket
in the *face* so the keys reached the outside; r5 has no window (the host lid is the front), so the
body drops into the deck's recess and its key face finishes flush with the deck's top face. The
seat is a ledge along each long wall at z 33.9, below the body's whole 11 mm column; the body's
underside on that ledge is a zero-volume tangent in the interference sweep, which is the intended
seat, not a collision. See "The keyboard is sunk into an open recess" above for the geometry.

**The OLED sits in a window over its glass, not behind the plate.** The module is measured: pcb
35.5 × 33.7 × 1.2, the glass on it 34.5 × 23 × 1.45, and the lit 128 × 64 area inside the glass
border 29.42 × 14.7. The deck is 4.5 mm, thicker than the module, so it carries two cuts: a
**36.8 × 25.3 window** through the top face (the glass plus 1.15 mm a side) and a **38.3 × 36.5
pocket** sunk 1.2 mm into the underside (the pcb plus 1.4 mm a side). Both grew by 1 mm a side
for fitting tolerance: 0.15 mm round the glass is less than the glass slab's own tolerance, and
the module drops in from below through a printed plate. The pcb drops in from below and stops on the pocket's ceiling, flush with
the deck's underside, so its back is exposed and can be taped across. The plate between the two
cuts is the bezel and the seat: with the window now wider than the pcb's 0.5 mm side border, the
sides carry nothing and the **4.2 mm end seats** hold the module; it cannot drop through, since the
window is 25.3 mm across a 33.7 mm board. Through the window you see the glass, its black border,
and a 0.65 mm sliver of the board at each side. The glass finishes 1.85 mm below the top face,
inside the window. Sizing one through-hole to the pcb — as this did earlier — left the board's edge
in view all round the panel and put a 35.8 × 34.8 hole in the plate for a 1.3" screen. r4's face was
the third arrangement (module behind a 26 × 15 window), which cannot be copied here because the
host lid, not the deck, is the front.

The window is centred on the pcb, not offset: the glass is 23 mm on a 33.7 mm board, so it leaves
5.35 mm of board at each end and the window sits 5.6 mm in from both ends of the pocket. It was
1.0 mm in from the hinge edge (9.7 mm at the header end), which put the hole 4.75 mm too far
towards the hinge — the board's ~5 mm borders are what told us, since 23 + 5 + 5 is the 33 mm
height the OLED board has.

The side seat is set by the hardware, not by choice: the pcb is only 0.5 mm wider than its glass
either side, so a window with real glass clearance is as wide as the board and the sides cannot
carry it; the ends do. `assert()` holds the end seats and rejects a window that is larger than the
pcb in both axes (which would drop the module through).

**Keyboard height.** The K06 body is 11 mm and it is sunk into the deck, so the keys finish level
with the deck's top face: the highest point of the cassette is therefore the deck's top face,
44.9 mm from its floor (40.4 rim + 4.5 deck), not 55.9. With the 8 mm pad that is 52.9 mm in a
74.4 mm base — 21.5 mm spare, and 37.2 mm between the key face and the display glass. Although the
keyboard (y 102.5–162.1) and display (y 33.67–144.43) footprints overlap, that vertical clearance
keeps the closed cover off the keys.

## Verify before printing

1. **Your host's actual interior.** Peli's published lid/bottom depths are nominal; mould draft
   and the O-ring channel mean the usable cavity at the rim is larger, not smaller. The deck is
   sized from two measurements — the published floor section (`HOST_L/W`) and the mouth
   (`HOST_TOP`, 250 × 193 on the received 1200) — interpolated linearly, then cut 1 mm a side at
   the deck's own height (`R5_DECK_W/L`, 243.3 × 186.8). If your case is straighter or rounder than
   that line, measure the cavity at the deck's underside (8 + 40.4 mm above the case floor) and
   override the plate: `-D R5_DECK_W=248 -D R5_DECK_L=191`. The paint masks and stencils read the
   same variables. `R5_DECK_CLR` is the 1 mm; `R5_DECK_R` (derived: the tray's R14 plus the
   overhang, 18.4 mm) is the corner — the offset the host's moulded corner takes under the same
   taper, and it still covers the tray rim. Note what the first print told us: the *mouth* is
   bigger than the plate. It did not say the plate can be mouth-sized — the plate has to pass the
   tapered cavity down to the tray rim, and a 250 × 193 plate is flush with the mouth.
2. **The display's module and insert pattern.** r5 uses the Waveshare 7inch DSI LCD (C):
   192.96 × 110.76 module, M3 inserts at the explicit `R5_DISP_HOLES` coordinates and unused
   58 × 49 M2.5 standoffs at `R5_DISP_POSTS`. If your panel differs, edit those arrays (and
   `R5_PANEL` / `R5_PANEL_DEPTH`) in `common.scad`; the plate and the cover have only those holes.
3. **The DSI cable routing.** The display's 15-pin FFC leaves the cover through the right-side
   hinge-edge notch (`R5_CABLE_W` wide, centred at `R5_CABLE_X = 140.60` from the panel's left
   edge). On the default host its centre is x 160.87, directly within the deck's hinge opening
   (53.4 × 16 mm, x 150–203.4) and the tray's 110 mm rim notch (x 61.75–171.75). Assertions keep
   an 8 mm cable half-width inside both openings on either host. The 4-pin 5 V/I²C lead is routed
   inside; use a lead longer than the included 6 cm.
   The **deck** opening is deliberately right-sided because a centred one would open over the UPS
   HAT (B) and its 8.4 V jack/USB-A/switch (x 84–118) and leave the Pi's cables under solid plate.
   Its right end stops 1 mm short of deck screw 5's countersink and 1 mm below the optional right
   fan. The lid notch uses the safe deck/tray overlap rather than moving the tray notch into that
   screw boss. Check the notch against your panel before printing.
   Cable length still matters: the stock 200 mm 22→15-way FFC has to cross a 74.4 mm deep case and
   reach a Pi that sits 10 mm from the hinge, so buy the 300 mm (or 500 mm) cable.
4. **The two measured envelopes, and the posts that follow from them.** The post tops are derived
   from the heights you measured (35 mm for the HAT (B) with cells, 30 mm for the Pi sandwich), so
   check the four numbers before printing the tray:

   | what | parameter | value now | if yours differs |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | HAT (B) pcb above its cells | `HAT_B_BELOW_R4` | 23.35 (Waveshare drawing) | set `R5_UPS_LIFT`; the UPS posts move with it |
   | BASE → Pi standoff | `R5_M2_GAP` | 11 (Seeed stud pack) | shorten it and the whole Pi sandwich drops |
   | air under the M.2 BASE | `R5_M2_CLR` | 5 | must clear the SSD (2.38) + connector |
   | air over the taller assembly | `R5_STACK_AIR` | 3 | sets `R5_H_BOT` = 40.4 |

   The Pi sandwich is derived bottom-up and `assert()`s that it stays inside your 30 mm envelope,
   so an impossible combination fails the build instead of printing a tray that clashes.
5. **The M.2 BASE hole pattern.** The Pi posts assume Seeed's BASE has the Pi's own 58 × 49
   pattern so one M2.5 per hole can pass through the BASE, its standoff and the Pi. Check the
   BASE you have; if its holes differ, the Pi posts need their own `R5_PI_HOLES`.
6. **The cell holders.** The two 18650s hang in Waveshare's soldered holders with their cans
   pointing down; the tray's cradles only locate them. Confirm the holder's 23.35 mm below-pcb
   figure on your board — if it is less, the cells float and the cradles must move up.
7. **The K06's corner radius.** `R5_KB_CORNER` = 6.4 mm is measured off the manual's outline
   drawing, not from the part. Check it against your keyboard: too small and the deck's corners
   stand inside the body's outline, too large and the body rattles. Nothing else depends on it —
   the pocket follows the radius, and the seat ledges start and stop clear of the corner arcs.
8. **The status OLED module.** The deck is cut for the measured module: pcb 35.5 × 33.7 × 1.2,
   glass 34.5 × 23 × 1.45, lit area 29.42 × 14.7. The glass is centred on the pcb — 0.5 mm a side
   across the width and 5.35 mm at each end along its length — so `R5_OLED_GLASS_Y` follows the
   three sizes above rather than being a separate guess. Its 4.2 mm end border is now the window's
   seat: the window carries 1 mm of tolerance a side, which is more than the pcb's 0.5 mm side
   border, so the sides do not carry the board. Check `R5_OLED_BOARD`, `R5_OLED_GLASS` and
   `R5_OLED_ACTIVE` against your board; the window and pocket follow, and `assert()`s guard the
   ends and the plate left over the glass. (If your module's glass is *not* centred — the older
   note here put it 1.0 mm in from the hinge edge — override `R5_OLED_GLASS_Y` with the measured
   inset; the window shifts with it.)
9. **The deck screws.** Seven **countersunk** M3 × 10 (DIN 7991 / ISO 10642) into heat-set inserts
   in the tray bosses — the deck's seats are 90° countersinks, not counterbores, because the deck
   prints top-face down and a flat counterbore ceiling is an unsupportable bridge. The inserts are
   the part that wears, so fit them with a soldering iron at ~230 °C and let them cool before the
   first screw. `M3_INS_D` = 4.2 mm is sized for a 4.6 mm-OD insert; a 5 mm-OD insert will crack
   the 2.4 mm of wall the Ø9 boss leaves, so check the insert you bought.
10. **The charge socket.** r5's 5.5 × 2.1 mm socket is in the deck, not the host wall: Ø9.5
    barrel, nut below, tags towards the Pi. Check that its thread clears the 4.5 mm plate plus
    the nut, that its lead reaches the HAT's 8.4 V jack with a service loop (the deck lifts off),
    and that you are happy to plug it in with the lid open. There is no case hole to drill.
