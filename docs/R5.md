# r5 — internal cassette in a host hard case

r5 keeps r4's electronics (Raspberry Pi 5, Touch Display 2, Waveshare UPS HAT (B) with 2× 18650,
Rii K06) and **deletes the air-quality sensor**. It replaces the printed exterior with an
internal cassette that drops into an off-the-shelf waterproof hard case, so the shell, the corner
bumpers, the grip ribs and the silicone corner mould all stop being printed parts.

```
host hard case (bought)
├─ base cavity ── foam pad ── r5_tray ── cells + HAT (B) + Pi on the floor
│                                └── r5_deck (screwed on top): keyboard + status OLED
└─ lid cavity  ── VHB tape ── r5_lid_plate ── Touch Display 2 (glass towards the base)
```

## The proposed host does not fit

The **Peli R40** cannot hold this hardware, and neither can the R60:

| Case | Interior L × W | Lid depth | Base depth | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R40** (proposed) | 193.8 × 119.4 | — (whole interior 48.3) | — | **Impossible** |
| **R60** | 226.1 × 136.4 | — (whole interior 75.2) | — | **Impossible** |
| Peli 1150 | 210.6 × 147.1 | 19.1 | 72.9 | Lid is 2 mm short of the display + plate + lead fold |
| Peli 1170 | 267.7 × 153.4 | 27.4 | 52.8 | Lid OK, but only 42.3 mm clear above the deck under the display |
| **Peli 1200** (default) | 236.5 × 181.1 | **30.2** | **74.4** | **Fits: 67.95 mm stack in a 74.4 mm base, 19.7 mm clear over the keyboard** |
| Peli 1400 | 300.0 × 225.3 | 30.0 | 101.6 | Fits with a lot of spare height (297 × 222 cassette) |

Sources: the Peli product pages (`pelican.com/us/en/product/cases/<model>`, interior + lid/bottom
depth) and the 2025 Peli catalogue (Ruck series interior sizes); figures converted from inches. The R40/R60 publish only a **total** depth, no
lid split — 48.3 mm and 75.2 mm.

Two independent blockers on the R40:

- **The display cannot go in the lid.** The Touch Display 2 is **14.92 mm** deep (6.42 panel +
  8.5 standoffs) and needs a 2 mm mounting plate: **16.92 mm**, plus clearance. The R40's whole
  interior is 48.3 mm, so its lid cannot be 20.9 mm (that is the minimum this geometry asserts).
  It publishes no lid depth at all, and the lid carries a snap-in TPU organizer.
- **The base cannot hold the cell stack.** Cells (18.4) + HAT holder (23.35 from its pcb) +
  pogo gap (6) + Pi pcb (1.6) + header clearance (9.5) is **42.5 mm of tray** before any pad, deck
  or keyboard; the closed cassette needs **67.95 mm**. The R40's *total* interior is 48.3 mm.

R60 fails the same way: 75.2 mm total cannot hold a 67.95 mm cassette in the base *and* a
16.92 mm display in the lid, and it publishes no lid split. Neither case is dimensioned for a
143.4 × 91.46 mm panel either — the R40 is a small-phone case.

**Peli 1200** is the recommendation: its 30.2 mm lid takes the 2 mm plate, the display's 8.5 mm
standoffs and the 6.42 mm panel (16.92 mm) with 13 mm left for the cable fold, and its 74.4 mm
base holds the cassette: 8 mm pad + 46.0 mm tray + 3 mm deck + 11 mm keyboard = 67.95 mm, leaving
6.45 mm. Interior 236.5 × 181.1 gives a **233.5 × 178.1 cassette** at 1.5 mm per side.
`HOST` also accepts `"1400"` (300 × 225.3 interior) if you want the extra headroom and volume.
The 1150/1170 rows are recorded only to show why they are not options; `HOST` rejects them by name.

## Parts

| File | Part | Print | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cad/r5_tray.scad` | floor tray | floor down | walls only, no exterior styling; M2.5 bosses for the deck |
| `cad/r5_deck.scad` | deck | as modelled, plate down | keyboard pocket + lip, OLED through-pocket with ledges, 40-pin/FFC keep-out |
| `cad/r5_lid_plate.scad` | display plate | flat | 4 × M2.5 into the display's standoffs; VHB taped to the lid |

`HOST` in `common.scad` selects `"1200"` (default) or `"1400"`; `assert()` fails the build if the
host's lid, base or width cannot take the stack, so an unsuitable host cannot be printed silently:
it rejects the display assembly (lid < 20.9 mm), the deck under the glass, the keyboard stack in
the base, and a cassette too narrow for the keyboard pocket.

```bash
python3 cad/tools/export.py --r5                 # the three cassette parts
openscad -D R5=true cad/assembly_r5.scad         # cassette + transparent host envelope
openscad -D R5=true -D 'CHECK="tray:deck"' cad/assembly_r5.scad   # one interference pair
```

## What changed against r4

- **No air-quality sensor.** `SENSOR_*` no longer applies in r5; the left wall is free. The
  firmware already degrades cleanly (BME688 not found → environment screen shows the absence and
  the environment alert never fires), so **no firmware change is required** for r5; the
  environment page simply has nothing to show and the auto-switch never selects it.
- **No exterior geometry.** Bumpers, ribs, edge chamfers, `outer_body()` and the charge plug are
  not printed. `docs/CORNER_MOULD.md` becomes irrelevant for r5.
- **The display moves to the lid.** In r4 the case posts carried the display and the Pi hung off
  it; in r5 the display hangs off a plate taped into the lid, and the Pi + HAT (B) stack stands on
  the tray floor. Nothing clamps the Pi: the deck has a keep-out over its 40-pin header and FFC
  connectors, because a pillar on the Pi's mounting holes would sit on the header (it is 3.5 mm
  from the GPIO edge, over those holes). The stack is held by Waveshare's own 6 mm Pi↔HAT
  standoffs and located laterally by the cell cradles.
- **Split plane disappears.** There is no printed split: the tray is 46.0 mm, the deck is a 3 mm
  plate on top, and the lid plate is independent.
- **Charge input moves to the right wall**, next to the HAT's own 8.4 V jack (it was in the top
  wall in r4, which is now the hinge side).

## Decisions and their risks

**The host must be drilled or the socket must be led out.** r5 needs the 8.4 V charge lead to
reach the outside. The tray carries a bulkhead socket on its right wall; the host wall needs a
matching hole (Ø9.5 + a 14 mm gasket seat) — that **breaks the host's IP rating** unless a proper
bulkhead connector is used. Alternative: leave the host sealed and run the lead through the
case's pressure-relief valve port (both cases have one), which also breaks the seal. If
waterproofing matters, use a sealed panel-mount barrel jack with its own O-ring and drill the
host wall; that is the recommended path and is what the tray geometry assumes.

**The lid plate is taped, not screwed.** Screwing it to the lid would put four holes through the
host's lid — worse for water resistance than tape, and the lid is a thin single-skin moulding
with no bosses. VHB tape on a clean, degreased lid face is strong enough for the panel's mass;
it is not removable without a new strip, hence the two finger slots.

**Heat.** The host is closed and sealed, and the Pi 5 idles around 5 W and peaks near 12 W. The
tray's floor vents only move air inside the host; there is no path through the host wall, and the
Peli's own pressure-relief valve is a 3 micron hydrophobic vent (it equalises pressure, it does
not convect). Expect the SoC to sit at its throttle point under sustained load. If that is not
acceptable, the honest options are a vented replacement for the relief-valve plug or a small
heatsink coupled to the host wall — not extra holes.

**The keyboard is only on the deck, so it is not usable with the case shut.** That matches r4:
the face window was the typing surface. With r5 you open the lid, the display is in the lid over
the keyboard. The K06's 2.4 GHz/BT link means the keyboard can be lifted out and used at a
distance from the deck if you want.

**The keyboard rests on the deck plate, keys up.** r4 dropped the K06 into a pocket in the *face*
so the keys reached the outside; r5 has no window (the host lid is the front), so the body simply
lies on the deck inside a low wall ring, trapped from above by a 2.5 mm lip over the Esc-row bezel
(the only edge with a bezel). The 151 × 59 body stands 11 mm proud of the deck. Its bottom face is
flush with the plate, which the interference sweep reports as a zero-volume tangent — that is the
intended seat, not a collision.

**The OLED sits in a through-pocket, not behind a window.** The 35 × 34 module is on the *outside*
of the deck, so the plate must be absent under it: a 35.8 × 34.8 hole with four 5.5 mm corner
ledges that carry it. r4's face had the opposite arrangement (module behind a 26 × 15 window in the
plate), and copying that here would have put the plate over the panel.

**Keyboard height.** The K06 body is 11 mm and its pocket walls stand on the 3 mm deck, so the
highest point of the cassette is 59.95 mm from its floor. With the 8 mm pad that is 67.95 mm in
a 74.4 mm base: 6.45 mm spare, and 19.73 mm between the keyboard's top face and the display
glass. The keyboard also sits at y 102.5–162.1, outside the closed display's footprint
(y 8–99.5), so nothing presses on the keys with the lid shut.

## Verify before printing

1. **Your host's actual interior.** Peli's published lid/bottom depths are nominal; mould draft
   and the O-ring channel mean the usable cavity at the rim is smaller. Measure the depth at the
   four corners and the width at the seal, then set `HOST_CLR` and check `HOST_BOT/LID`.
2. **The display's outer standoff pattern.** r5 uses the r4 value (122.74 × 72.96). If your TD2
   differs, edit `R5_DISP_HOLES` — the plate has only those four holes.
3. **The DSI/GPIO cable routing.** The display's FFC and GPIO leads run from the lid plate, past
   the hinge, and up to the Pi through the deck's hinge-side opening (89 × 17 mm over the Pi's
   connector edge) and the tray's 120 mm rim notch. Cable length decides whether this works: the
   stock 200 mm 22→15-way FFC has to cross a 74.4 mm deep case and reach a Pi that sits 10 mm from
   the hinge, so buy the 300 mm (or 500 mm) cable.
4. **HAT (B) standoff height.** `UPS_COMP` (6 mm) is the pogo gap. The tray is cut to the stack
   height that follows from it (`R5_H_BOT` = 46.0 mm), and the deck sits on the tray rim: if your
   HAT sits taller, the deck will press on the Pi's header. Assemble the Pi + HAT + cells and
   measure the stack before printing the deck.
5. **The cell holders.** The two 18650s hang in Waveshare's soldered holders with their cans
   pointing down; the tray's cradles only locate them. Confirm the holder's 23.35 mm below-pcb
   figure on your board — if it is less, the cells float and the cradles must move up.
